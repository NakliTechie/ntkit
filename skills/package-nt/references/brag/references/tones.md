# Tone reference

Seven tones. Each tone changes pacing, typography and transition style. The words follow one rule in every tone.

## The rule for on-screen words, every tone

Every line of on-screen text is either a name (the product name, a feature name, a number with its unit) or a full sentence with a subject and a verb. A tone changes how big, how fast and how loud the words are, never whether they form sentences. The repo's `ATTEST.md` section 4.4 lists the shapes to avoid; in video text the common ones are:

- verbless fragments for rhythm ("Obvious mistake.", "One file. No server.");
- a denial of a view nobody stated, followed by a reveal;
- a closing tagline written to be quoted rather than to say what the product does;
- a metaphor in place of what the product does.

Test each line by deleting it. If the viewer loses no fact about the product, cut it. A product name alone on its title card is a name, so it passes. Humour comes from the product and from the delivery, not from fragment cadence.

## `default`

**Energy:** Playful, clean, postable. The product gets to be funny on its own terms.

**Voice:** First person plural, warm and direct, with no corporate language.

**Typography:** Mixed case. Comfortable weight. Let words breathe.

**Pacing:** 4-5 scenes. Each scene 3-5 seconds. Comfortable rhythm.

**Hook style:** A simple observation, in one full sentence, that sets up the reveal.
```
Dating apps were built for humans, and horses were left out.
```

**Highlight style:** One feature per scene, in one short sentence.
```
You swipe through eligible horses near your pasture.
```

**Outro style:** The product name, then one sentence that says what it does.
```
Horse Tinder.
It finds your horse a stablemate.
```

**Transitions:** Crossfade or clean slide.

**When to use:** Most absurd consumer apps. Projects that have personality without trying too hard.

## `polished`

**Energy:** Serious and elegant. Restraint is the creative choice.

**Voice:** Third person, or the product's own screens with captions.

**Typography:** Mixed case. Light-to-medium weight. Generous letter-spacing. Nothing aggressive.

**Pacing:** 3-4 scenes. Each scene 4-6 seconds. Slow reveals.

**Hook style:** A single strong image or the product name at full scale.

**Highlight style:** One feature per scene, in one sentence. No bullets or lists.
```
Every pilot gets a wing certificate at the end of the course.
```

**Outro style:** The product name, then one sentence that says what it does, then a held empty frame.

**Transitions:** Slow crossfade (0.6-0.8s).

**When to use:** Projects that aren't jokes. Products that want to feel premium. Anytime the user says "clean" or "elegant."

## `yc-parody`

**Energy:** Deadpan startup launch. The joke is how seriously it's delivered.

**Voice:** Serious and matter-of-fact, with no winking. The absurdity comes from the product, not the tone.

**Typography:** Sentence case. Heavy weight or medium weight. Courier-adjacent for data points. No decoration.

**Pacing:** 4-5 scenes. Structured. Each scene makes one claim.

**Hook style:** The problem, stated seriously in full sentences.
```
Every day, taxis carry us.
Nobody carries the taxis.
```

**Highlight style:** A feature or metric, stated as a sentence or as a number with its unit.
```
We operate in 12 cities.
Fleet uptime: 99.1%
```

**Outro style:** The product name, one sentence that says what it does, and a URL.
```
Taxi for Taxis
It is a ride-hailing app for taxis.
taxifortaxis.com
```

**Transitions:** Hard cut or minimal crossfade (0.2s).

**When to use:** Any recursive or absurd concept that benefits from being played straight. "Psychologists for Chatbots", "Taxi for Taxis", "Briefcase for Baby."

---

## `chaotic`

**Energy:** Fast, loud, unhinged. The video is the joke.

**Voice:** Loud short sentences and bare numbers, one per beat. No exclamation marks; the size and speed carry the volume.

**Typography:** ALL CAPS. Heavy weight. Slightly oversized. Some words tilted. Some words larger than expected.

**Pacing:** 6-8 scenes. Some scenes under 2 seconds. Never more than 4 seconds per scene.

**Hook style:** Something that shouldn't exist, stated as one loud sentence.
```
TRANSPORTATION WAS TOO CALM, SO WE ADDED BOARS.
```

**Highlight style:** Rapid-fire. One number with its unit, or one short sentence, per beat.
```
8,400 BOARS ON CALL
PICKUP IN 3 MINUTES
THE BOAR ARRIVES TUSKS FIRST.
```

**Outro style:** The name slams in, one sentence says what it does, then cut to black.
```
UBER FOR WILD BOARS
You book a wild boar, and it comes to you.
```

**Transitions:** Hard cut. Flash cut (brief white/black frame). Zoom cut (scale 1.2→1.0 on entrance).

**When to use:** Chaotic concepts, logistics parodies, anything that can be played as a hype reel. "Uber for Wild Boars", anything with urgency or speed.

---

## `deadpan`

**Energy:** Calm and dry. The joke is that nothing registers as unusual.

**Voice:** Minimal: one quiet sentence per scene, then the product.

**Typography:** Mixed case. Large. Sparse. Lots of empty space. One thought at a time.

**Pacing:** 3-4 scenes. Long holds. 4-7 seconds per scene. The pace is the joke.

**Hook style:** A quiet observation in one sentence, with no setup.
```
I used to fear the sky.
```

**Highlight style:** One sentence per scene, with no bullets and no excitement.
```
Now I fear birds, weather and gravity.
```

**Outro style:** The product name, and at most one small sentence that says what it does. Long hold on empty space.
```
Fish Flight School.
```

**Transitions:** Very slow crossfade (0.8-1.0s), or a long hold before the next scene.

**When to use:** Projects whose strongest material is a quote or testimonial, such as the "Psychologists for Chatbots" site. Anything where a slow pace makes the joke work.

---

## `cinematic`

**Energy:** Dramatic and trailer-scale. The product is treated like a blockbuster.

**Voice:** Short, serious, declarative sentences, one per scene.

**Typography:** ALL CAPS or heavy mixed case. Full-bleed scenes. Large type. Significant scale.

**Pacing:** 4-5 scenes. 3-5 seconds each. Dramatic reveals, not quick cuts.

**Hook style:** A sweeping statement about the world, in one serious sentence.
```
For too long,
fish were told to stay underwater.
```

**Highlight style:** The product's capabilities, one sentence each.
```
Students learn to find rising warm air.
They learn to steer through clouds.
They practise emergency splash landings.
```

**Outro style:** The product name full-screen, then one sentence that says what it does. Music swell implied.
```
FISH FLIGHT SCHOOL
It teaches fish to fly.
```

**Transitions:** Dramatic wipe or slow crossfade with scale (scene enters at 0.95, reaches 1.0).

**When to use:** Anything with natural epic quality, such as "Fish Flight School". Anything involving scale, nature, or grand claims.

---

## `app-store`

**Energy:** Clean, professional, feature-forward. The product is presented as real, even when it's not.

**Voice:** Feature and benefit, in the third person and the present tense.

**Typography:** Title case for feature names, sentence case for descriptions. Medium weight. Clean and readable.

**Pacing:** 4-6 scenes. Each scene shows a feature name, then one or two sentences about it.

**Hook style:** The product name, then one sentence that says what it does.
```
Psychologists for Chatbots.
It gives chatbots therapy after hard conversations.
```

**Highlight style:** A feature card: the feature name, then one sentence.
```
Prompt Trauma Processing
It finds harmful conversation patterns and helps the chatbot recover.
```

**Outro style:** One sentence that tells the viewer what to do or what is available.
```
It is available now for every chatbot model.
```

**Transitions:** Clean slide or wipe (0.35-0.45s). Nothing dramatic.

**When to use:** Products that benefit from being taken seriously as a product, even if absurd. Good for anything B2B-parody or therapy/wellness adjacent.
